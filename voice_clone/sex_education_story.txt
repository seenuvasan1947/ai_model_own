Ravi and Meera had been married for six months, and they loved each other deeply. But there was one subject they never talked about, because both of them felt awkward and shy.

One evening, Meera finally said, "I think we should talk about it. Not because something is wrong, but because we should both feel safe and comfortable."

Ravi nodded. "You are right. Honestly, I have many questions too."

They decided to visit a doctor together. The doctor welcomed them, and said, "Good, most people never ask, and they only find out the answers when there is a problem."

She began with communication. "The most important thing between partners is honest talk. Say what you like, what you do not like, and what you are worried about. Nobody can read your mind."

Then she spoke about consent. "Consent must be clear and given freely, every single time. Being married or being in a relationship does not change this. And it can be taken back at any moment. Silence is not a yes."

Next came health. "Protection matters. Condoms help prevent both unplanned pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. If you are not planning a child, talk about contraception together, and choose a method that suits you both. Regular check-ups and tests are a normal part of staying healthy, and there is no shame in them."

She also added, "If something hurts, or does not feel right, tell your doctor. Many problems are common, and most of them can be treated."

On the way home, Ravi held Meera's hand, and said, "I feel much lighter now."

Meera smiled. "Me too. Talking was the hardest part."

Remember, knowing about your body and your health is nothing to be ashamed of. This story is for education only, so if you have questions, please ask a qualified doctor.
