# Version 3: foundations first — numbers, word meaning, grammar, and retelling

Branch `hebbian-v3`, cut from `hebbian-v2` / `release-v1.0.0` (commit `fdf9e53`), which are untouched. Agreed plan: teach the foundations
in school order and measure each stage. **All five stages are done** (below), with the measured results and the gaps.

| Stage | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | An understood question with no answer says "i do not know" instead of a wrong-relation guess | `workspace.py` |
| 1–2 | **Number sense and arithmetic, learned**: counting, counting-on addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, practice | `numbers.py` |
| 3 | **Word meaning**: events with roles (when / where / why / with / how long / how many), named things, core everyday meanings | `roles.py`, `meanings.py`, `resources/core_meanings.tsv` |
| 4 | **Grammar as usable knowledge**: word forms and classes on request; one fact told in the active, passive, negative, future and question forms | `grammar.py` |
| 5 | **Retelling in its own words** from the stored facts, with pronouns, chronology and voice changes | `Workspace.retell`, `rephrase` |

## 0. Wrong-relation fix
When a question was understood but nothing is stored, v2 let the language layer guess, which could answer a different relation ("what
color is the lion ?" -> "the lion says roar"). Now only questions the rules cannot read at all may use it. A test covers it.

## 1–2. Learned number sense (`numbers.py`)
Counting is learned from counting runs ("zero one two ...", "ten comes after nine"): each neighbouring pair strengthens a digit->next-digit
synapse and, when a digit wraps (9 -> 0), a *carry* link. The same digit links serve every place, so counting to a hundred gives the next
number of anything. Adding is counting on from the larger number, subtracting is counting back, multiplying is repeated adding, dividing is
repeated subtracting. A sum worked out twice (or taught: "seven plus eight is fifteen .") is *recalled* from then on; traces fade, and the
skill falls back to counting. A wrong claim ("two plus two is five .") loses to counting. Given as vocabulary, not learned: number words and
positional notation. Questions: `what is 27 + 15`, `... divided by ...`, `what comes after / before 39`, `count from 3 to 8`, `which is bigger , 7 or 12`.
Run `python train.py` (or `Workspace.school()`) to send a workspace to school; before that it falls back to the old rule and says so.

Measured (benchmark J; exact Python arithmetic is only the answer key): counting taught to 19 or more gives the next number for everything up to
2,000 (taught only to 9: 90%, failing exactly on numbers ending in 9, and saying so); after school 200 random problems per operation are all
right. That exactness is by construction (it is built from correct successor links). Counting steps for a two-digit sum: 36.6 the first two
times, 0 from the third (recalled). A wrong fact taught ten times does not change the answer.

## 3. Meaning: events, roles, named things, core vocabulary
* **Roles.** A verb phrase keeps its adjuncts as roles on the fact: `the mutiny started in Meerut on 10 May 1857` is one fact with
  `where = in meerut`, `when = on 10 may 1857`; `greased with cow fat` -> `with`; `angry about the cartridges` -> `about`; `burned for four days`
  -> `duration`; `More than 1500 people died` -> `n`; `refused to use them` -> `inf`; `After the mutiny the Crown took control` -> `when = after the mutiny`.
  When / where / why / how long / how many / "what happened in 1858" / "what happened after the mutiny" / "who refused to use X" are answered from
  these roles, and the answer is a whole sentence built from the fact ("the sepoy mutiny started in meerut on 10 may 1857 .").
* **Named things.** "the Great Fire of London", "Apollo 11", "Saturn V", "Sea of Tranquility" are single names; "the fire" / "the mutiny" later
  refer back to them; "It" finds the named thing; "the baker, Thomas Farriner," makes them one person; "The airplane was called the Wright Flyer"
  makes two names for one thing; "made X their leader" makes X the leader; an object pronoun never refers to its own clause's subject.
* **Core meanings.** A hand-written table of 114 everyday nouns (`resources/core_meanings.tsv`): what a thing is used for, made of, made up of,
  where it is found. "what is a spoon used for ?", "what is a bridge made of ?", "what does a car have ?", "where is a bridge found ?". What the
  user tells it ("a spoon is used for stirring tea") wins over the table; for words not in the table it says "i do not know".
* The bundled dictionary grew from 388 to 522 words (people, events, places, verbs of history and travel). **Note:** those words were chosen with
  the passages below in mind, so results with the bundled dictionary alone are not a clean held-out test. WordNet (`python fetch_wordnet.py`)
  is independent of the passages and is the fair setting.

## 4. Grammar as knowledge
Asked: "what is the plural of mouse ?" (mice), "the past tense of go ?" (went), "the past participle of eat ?" (eaten), "the comparative of big ?"
(bigger), "what kind of word is run ?" (a noun or a verb), "is quickly an adverb ?". Used: `Workspace.rephrase("The dog chased the cat.")` gives
the passive (`the cat was chased by the dog`), negative, future and question forms, by rule (agreement, tense, participle, a/an); stative verbs
(last, become, have ...) are not turned into passives. The language layer still learns grammar from examples; these rules make it usable on
demand and let the answers be worded differently.

## 5. Retelling
`retell(topic)` / "tell me about X" / `/retell` in the chat: from the stored facts only, in order of time, a pronoun for a subject just named
("the great fire of london began on 2 september 1666 . it started in a bakery ..."), two or more events folded together, some sentences in the
passive in the varied style, never a fact that was not read. "refused to use the cartridge", "destroyed 13000 houses", "for four days" survive.

## Measured on short factual passages (`evaluate_story.py`, 7 passages, 123 questions asked from different angles; WordNet dictionary)
Each question lists the keywords a right answer must contain. This judge is lenient: it checks keywords, not grammar, and some "right" replies
are awkward ("the armstrongs step_onto the first" was counted right in an earlier run). Baseline = the code of the previous stage (commit `6e6697d`).

| Passage | role | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sepoy Mutiny (27 questions) | development: fixes were made on its failures | 14 | **27** |
| Great Fire of London (20) | development | 9 | **17** |
| Titanic (16) | development | 8 | **14** |
| Apollo 11 (16) | held out when first run, then used for fixes | 8 | first look **9**, now 11 |
| Wright brothers (15) | held out when first run, then used for fixes | 5 | first look **9**, now 13 |
| Mount Everest (14) | held out when first run, then used for fixes (second round) | 5 | first look **7**, now 11 |
| Marie Curie (15) | **clean held-out**: written before the second round of fixes, run once | 2 | **5** (6 after a pronoun fix made afterwards) |

The honest reading: on passages the fixes were made on, answers went from about half to nearly all. On passages never seen before, the first-look
results were 9/16 (Apollo), 9/15 (Wright), 7/14 (Everest) and 5/15 (Curie), against 8, 5, 5 and 2 for the previous stage: **about 40–55% of
questions answered usefully, roughly 2–3 more per passage than before** (and not every reply was well-formed). The rules generalise a little, not a
lot: every new passage shows constructions that are not handled (Curie: "to study physics" as a reason, "she moved to Paris in 1891" with a
role in the question, "they discovered two new elements" through a pronoun that stands for two people, "died in a street accident"). The second
round of fixes (made on Everest: subject phrases like "X from Y and Z from W", double-object verbs, "they" for a pair, names after a noun) raised
Everest from 7 to 11 but did **not** move the unseen Curie passage (5 before and after), which is the clearer sign of how far these hand-written
fixes carry.

Retelling (all seven passages, WordNet): key facts present 11/11, 10/11, 8/9, 7/9, 7/10, 4/7, 4/8; **invented sentences 0** in every passage; word for word
copies 1–5 of 9–15 sentences; grammar issues found by the rule checker 0–1 (unknown names ignored). Not measured: whether a person finds the retelling
readable. Reading it, there are errors: "the people was known to die", "they made bahadur shah zafar" (the leader is lost), a lost modifier ("officers"
for "british officers").

## Gaps that remain
* The reader is still rules; ellipsis, nested clauses, double-object verbs, long lists and most idioms are not handled; the second held-out passage
  shows that new constructions cost answers.
* No column arithmetic (big sums are refused beyond 20,000 counting steps), no negatives, fractions, decimals or word problems; numbers are not yet
  joined to the fact memory.
* The core meaning table has 114 nouns and no verbs; words outside it get "i do not know".
* The bundled dictionary alone is weak on unseen text (7/14 on Everest with WordNet; much less without).
* The workspace in `release/v1.0/` was built before v3; run `python train.py` to build one with the number sense and the larger dictionary.
