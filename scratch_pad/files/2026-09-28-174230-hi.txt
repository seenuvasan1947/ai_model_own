  The honest problem — even with 1 million sentences, pure Hebbian co-occurrence
  will still produce weak generation. Why:

  - It only learns which words appear near each other — not grammar structure,
    not sentence flow, not meaning composition
  - With free vocabulary (9000+ words), the model always gravitates toward the
    most frequent words ("the", "of", "a") no matter how much data you add —
    because those words genuinely appear next to everything

  The real gap is architecture, not data size. More data alone won't fix it.

  What actually fixes generation quality — two real options:

  Option A: Add POS tagging from NLTK (auto, not hand-labeled)
  NLTK can auto-tag every word in Brown as NOUN/VERB/ADJ/etc.
  Use those tags as generation constraints — same as the toy model but learned 
  automatically from real corpus.
  This keeps your "no backprop" principle and gives grammatically constrained
  generation. This is the most practical next upgrade.

  Option B: Add a frozen pretrained transformer just for generation
  Hebbian layer = what you continuously learn (domain knowledge)
  Frozen transformer (small GPT-2) = handles fluent sentence generation
  Combined: Hebbian picks the topic/domain words, transformer makes them fluent
  This is the RAG-style architecture from the conversation — best of both.

  My recommendation: Option A first — it's pure Python, no new dependencies
