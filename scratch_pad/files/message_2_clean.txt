**How the brain likely learns (instead of backprop)**

1. **Hebbian learning** — "Neurons that fire together, wire together." When one neuron helps trigger another, the connection (synapse) between them gets strengthened. This is a *local* rule — each synapse adjusts based only on the activity of the two neurons it connects, not some global error signal sent backward through the whole network.

2. **Synaptic plasticity** — Connections get physically stronger or weaker over time (more/less neurotransmitter release, more/fewer receptors). This is the biological version of "updating weights," but it happens locally and chemically, not through calculus-based gradients.

3. **Neuromodulators** — Chemicals like dopamine act as a kind of reward/error signal. When something goes better or worse than expected, dopamine spikes or dips, and this nudges nearby synapses to strengthen or weaken. Some researchers see this as the brain's rough analog to a "learning signal" — but it's broadcast chemically, not computed and routed backward like backprop.

4. **Predictive processing** — There's a theory (this one's genuinely influential in neuroscience) that the brain is constantly predicting what will happen next — next sound, next word, next sensation — and learning happens when reality doesn't match the prediction. That mismatch ("prediction error") drives adjustment. This is philosophically similar to next-word prediction in LLMs, but the *mechanism* is totally different — it's distributed, chemical, and local, not a mathematical gradient computed across layers.

**Does the brain "predict the next word" like an LLM?**
Sort of, but not that literally. When you listen to speech, your brain does actively predict upcoming words (that's why you notice typos or finish others' sentences) — this is well-documented. But it's not doing anything like tokenization or probability distributions over a vocabulary. It's more like layered expectation based on context, memory, and meaning.

**The honest scientific answer**
Nobody has fully cracked how memory storage + learning + prediction integrate in the brain. We know the ingredients (synapses, neuromodulators, predictive coding, hierarchical processing in cortex) but not the full recipe or algorithm. Backprop is a human-invented trick that works great on computers but almost certainly isn't what biological brains do — it would require precise, symmetric, backward-flowing signals that don't have a known biological mechanism.
